\parit{Proof.}
Choose a point $p\in\Omega$ and a chart at $p$.

Assume that the main theorem holds for some dimension $d$.
Then the components $g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j)$ of any Riemannian metric can be written as
\[
\langle \partial_i w, \partial_j w \rangle = g_{ij},
\eqlbl{eq:<ww>=g}
\]
for some smooth map $w\:\Omega \to \RR^d$.

There are $\tbinom{n+1}{2}$ components $g_{ij}$, each of which is a smooth function on~$\Omega$.
The equation \ref{eq:<ww>=g} has $d$ unknowns; namely, the coordinate functions of~$w$.
This suggests that $d$ should be at least $\tbinom{n+1}{2}$.
The remaining proof is very standard, we reduce the problem to an algebraic system between the \emph{jet spaces} of $w$ and $g$ at the point $p$; the jet space corresponds to the space of Taylor polynomials of a given degree at~$p$.

Denote by $J^m(g)$ the degree-$m$ Taylor polynomial of $g$ at~$p$.
This polynomial has $\tbinom{m+n}{n}$ coefficients in an $\tbinom{n+1}{2}$-dimensional space.
(Each coefficient corresponds to a certain partial derivative of $g$ of order at most $m$ at $p$.)
Such polynomials form an open subset of a $\tbinom{n+1}{2} \cdot \tbinom{m+n}{n}$-dimensional space.

Similarly, let $J^m(w)$ be the degree-$m$ Taylor polynomial of $w$ at~$p$.
This polynomial has $\tbinom{m+n}{n}$ coefficients in a $d$-dimensional space;
hence these polynomials form a space of dimension $d \cdot \tbinom{m+n}{n}$.

The equations \ref{eq:<ww>=g} define a smooth map $J^{m+1}(w)\mapsto J^m(g)$.
In other words, any partial derivative of $g$ of degree at most $m$ can be expressed in terms of partial derivatives of $w$ of degree at most $m+1$.
Since the system \ref{eq:<ww>=g} admits a solution for any metric $g$, there exists a $J^{m+1}(w)$ mapping to any given $J^m(g)$.
By Sard's lemma,
\[
d \cdot \tbinom{m+n+1}{n} \ge \tbinom{n+1}{2} \cdot  \tbinom{m+n}{n}.
\]
Since
$\tbinom{m+n+1}{n}/\tbinom{m+n}{n} \to 1$ as  $m \to \infty$, we conclude that $d \ge \tbinom{n+1}{2}$.
\qeds

This proof is taken from \cite[Appendix I]{gromov-rokhlin} and it works for general system of partial differential equations.

The system of equations \ref{eq:<ww>=g} makes perfect sense for $C^1$-smooth maps $w$, but the proof essentially uses that $w$ is $C^m$-smooth for sufficiently large $m$.
Without this assumption, the statement does not hold.

Indeed, the Nash--Kuiper theorem \cite{nash-1954,kuiper-1955} states that equation \ref{eq:<ww>=g} admits a $C^1$-smooth solution $w$ if $d = n+1$.
This bound is optimal, since if $d=n$, then the metric $g$ must be flat, which is not the case in general.
Notice that for $d = n+1$, the system \ref{eq:<ww>=g} is overdetermined, yet still admits a $C^1$-smooth solution.

It might happen that the optimal dimension for $C^2$-embeddings is smaller than $\tbinom{n+1}{2}$;
at least the above argument gives a worse bound in this case.
This question mentioned by Mikhael Gromov and Vladimir Rokhlin \cite[]{gromov-rokhlin}.

\section{Remarks}

Let us mention a closely related result that shows a huge difference between $C^1$ and $C^2$ isometric embeddings.
For example, it implies that the unit sphere admits $C^1$-smooth isometric embedding into an arbitrarily small ball in Euclidean 3-space.
There is no such $C^2$-embedding since Gauss curvature of the unit sphere is 1, but at an extremal point it must be at least $\tfrac1{r^2}$, where $r$ is the radius of the ball.

\begin{thm}{Nash--Kuiper theorem}
Let $(\Omega ,g)$ be a $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold and $f\:(\Omega ,g)\to \RR^q$ be a strictly short smooth regular map.
Suppose that $q\ge n+1$.
Then for any $\eps>0$ there is an $C^1$-smooth length-preserving maps $f_\eps\:(\Omega ,g)\to \RR^q$ that is $\eps$ close to $f$;
that is, $|f_\eps(x)-f(x)|<\eps$ for any $x\in \Omega$.

Moreover if $f$ is an embedding then we can assume that so is $f_\eps$.

\end{thm}


It was originally proved by John Nash \cite{nash-1954} with the condition $q\z\ge n+2$ and improved to $q\ge n+1$ by Nicolaas Kuiper \cite{kuiper-1955}.
The original proof uses Nash's twist in a different way.
Both papers are reader-friendly.

The discussed results lie in the foundations of the so-called \emph{homotopy principle}, or \emph{h-principlee}; an excellent introduction is given in the book by Yakov Eliashberg and Nikolai Mishachev \cite{eliashberg-mishachev}.

Many related questions are open.
For example, it is unknown if \textit{a neighborhood of any point in 2-dimensional Riemannian manifold admits a smooth length-preserving embedding into $\RR^3$}.







\begin{thm}{Nash's lemma}\label{lem:nash}
Let $f$ be a free map from a smooth manifold $\Omega$ to $\RR^q$.
Then for any metric tensor $h$ on $\Omega$ there is unique vector field $w=M(h)$ in $\N\Omega$ such that 
\[2\cdot Q(f,w)=h.\]
In other words, the linear operator $L\:w\mapsto 2\cdot Q(f,w)$
has a unique right inverse $M$
such that if $\langle M(h),\partial_if\rangle=0$ for any local cart and any $i$.

Moreover, 
\[\|Mh\|_{2,k}\le C\cdot\|h\|_{2,k}\]
for some fixed constant $C$.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Chose local coordinates in $\Omega$.
For any smooth map $w\:\Omega\to\RR^q$, we have
\begin{align*}
L_{ij}(w)&=2\cdot Q_{ij}(f,w)=
\\
&=\langle\partial_if,\partial_jw\rangle+\langle\partial_jf,\partial_iw\rangle=
\\
&=\partial_j\langle\partial_if,w\rangle+\partial_i\langle\partial_jf,w\rangle -2\cdot \langle\partial_{ij}f,w\rangle.
\end{align*}

Now choose a metric tensor $h$ on $\Omega$.
Since all $\partial_if$,
$\partial_{ij}f$
are linearly independent,
the following identities define uniquely  a field $w$ in $\T^2\Omega$
\begin{align*}
\langle\partial_{i}f,w\rangle&=0,
&
\langle\partial_{ij}f,w\rangle&=h_{ij}
\end{align*}
for all $i$ and $j$.

The identities $\langle\partial_{i}f,w\rangle=0$ hold if and only if $w(p)\in \N_p$ for any $p\z\in \Omega$.
Further, note that $w(p)$ does not depend on the choice of local coordinates,
and it defines the needed right inverse $M\:h\mapsto w$.
\qeds

Let $\Omega$ be a smooth manifold.
Given two $C^1$-maps $v,w\:\Omega \to\RR^q$, set 
\[g(\vec x,\vec y)=\tfrac12\cdot[\langle d v(\vec x),dw(\vec y)\rangle+\langle d v(\vec y),dw(\vec x)\rangle]\]
Note that $Q\:(v,w)\mapsto g$ is a bilinear operator and $Q(v,v)$ is the metric induced by $v\:\Omega \to\RR^q$.

We need to solve the quadratic equation
\[Q(v,v)=g\]
for a given smooth Riemannian metric $g$ on $\Omega$.





In the proof we will use the following weaker statement.
Suppose $v$ is free and $Q(v,v)=g$.
Then for any $C^{k,\alpha}$ metric tensor $h$ and all small $t>0$ there is $C^{k,\alpha}$ map 
$w\:\Omega \to \RR^q$ such that
\[Q(v+w,v+w)=g+t\cdot h,\]
or equivalently
\[L(w)+Q(w,w)=t\cdot h,\]
where $L=2\cdot Q(v,w)$ which is a linear operator.



Note that there is a bilinear operator $Q$ that takes two $C^1$-maps $v,w\:\Omega \to\RR^q$ and returns a metric tensor on $\Omega$ such that $Q(v,v)$ is the metric induced by $v\:\Omega \to\RR^q$.


Let $\Omega$ be a bounded domain in $\RR^n$ with smooth boundary $\partial\Omega$.
Fix a free embedding $f\:\RR^n\to\RR^q$.

Let $g=Q(f,f)$ and $h$ be another metric tensor on $\Omega$.

Consider another smooth map $w\:\Omega\to \RR^q$.
Note that $f+w$ induce $g+h$ if and only if
\[h=L(w)+Q(w,w),\eqlbl{eq:L+Q}\]
where $L(w)=2\cdot Q(f,w)$;
note that $L$ is linear.

Suppose we have another bilinear opearator $\tilde Q$ such that 
$L\tilde Q=Q$, so the equation \ref{eq:L+Q} can be reqritten as

Indeed, applying $L$ to both sides of this equation we get  \ref{lem:nash}.

If $f$ is free, than we say that $\Omega$ is a \emph{free submanifold}.

The space spanned by the first and second partial derivatives of $f$ at $p$ will be denoted by $\T^2_p=\T^2_p\Omega$.

Recall that second fundamental form $S$ is a field of symmetric quadratic forms on $\T \Omega$  with values in $\N \Omega$ that defined by

where $\nabla$ and $\bar \nabla$ denote the Levi-Civita connection on $\Omega$ and the ambient manifold;
in this particular case, $\bar \nabla$ is defined by the parallel translations on the Euclidean space.
Observe that the values of $S$ lie in binormal bundle $\BN \Omega$.

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:binormal}
Suppose that $\Omega$ is a free submanifold of $\RR^q$.
Show that for any metric tensor $h$ on $\Omega$ there is a unique binormal field $\vec n$ such  that 
\[h(\vec x,\vec y)=\langle S(\vec x,\vec y),\vec n\rangle\]
for any vector fields $\vec x,\vec y$ on $\Omega$.

Given $h$, consider the one-parameter family of maps $f_t\:\Omega \to \RR^q$ defined by 
\[f_t(p)= p+t\cdot \vec n(p);\]
let $g(t)$ be the metric tensor induced by $f_t$.
Show that $g'(0)=2\cdot h$.
\end{thm}

The exercise says that a free embedding can be perturbed so that the induced metric tensor moves in a given direction $h$.
Note that freeness of embedding is an \emph{open condition};
namely, if $\Omega$ is a free compact submanifold then any $C^2$-close embedding of $\Omega$ is free as well.
One may think that these two properties \emph{easily} imply the following statement,
but that is not at all easy;
it is a consequence of a deep result --- the so-called Nash--Moser theorem \cite{moser-1961}.
A simplified proof was obtained by Matthias G\"{u}nther \cite{gunter}.

\begin{thm}{Perturbation theorem}\label{thm:perturbation}
Let $f\:\Omega \hookrightarrow\RR^q$ be a free embedding, $g$ is the Riemannian metric induced by $f$ and $h$ is another metric tensor on $\Omega$.
Then for any $t$ sufficiently close to $0$, there is a free embedding of $f_t\:\Omega \hookrightarrow\RR^q$ with  induced metric tensor $g+t\cdot h$.  
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof of \ref{thm:nash-reformulation} modulo the perturbation theorem.}
Choose a free embedding $f\: \Omega \hookrightarrow\RR^s$;
it exists by \ref{ex:free-composition}.
Denote by $g_0$ its induced metric.

Scaling down $f$ if necessary, we can assume that $g>g_0$;
that is the metric tensor $\bar g= g-g_0$ is Riemannian.

Applying the approximation theorem (\ref{thm:approx}) we get a one parameter family of maps $F_\eps\:\Omega \to \RR^q$ with induced metrics $\bar g+\eps^2\cdot h$ for a fixed metric tensor $h$.

By the perturbation theorem (\ref{thm:perturbation}) there is a one parameter family of embedding $f_t\:\Omega \to \RR^s$ with induced metric $g_0+t\cdot h$.

Choose sufficiently small $\eps>0$ so that $f_t$ is defined for $t=-\eps^2$.
Consider the map $\Omega \to \RR^s\times\RR^q$ defined by 
$x\mapsto (f_t(x),F_\eps(x))$.
Observe that the induced metric of this map is
\[g_0+t\cdot h+\bar g+\eps^2\cdot h=g.\]
\qedsf

The induced metric tensor for a map to a pseudoeuclidean space can be defined the same way.

\begin{thm}{Theorem}
Any metric tensor $g$ on a compact smooth manifold $\Omega$ is induced by a smooth map $f\:\Omega \to \RR^{r,s}$ for some positive integers $r$ and $s$;
in other words, 
\[g=(df_1)^2+\dots+(df_r)^2-(df_{r+1})^2+\dots+(df_{r+s})^2\]
for some smooth functions $f_1,\dots,f_{r+s}$ on $\Omega$.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Note that any metric tensor on $\Omega$ can be expressed as a difference of two Riemannian tenors.
Therefore we can assume that $g$ is Riemannian.

Suppose that $\bm{F}_\eps\:\Omega \to \RR^{2\cdot q}$ and $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_{q}$ are provided by the approximation theorem (\ref{thm:approx}).
Consider the map $\bm{\phi}\:\Omega \to \RR^q$  with coordinate functions $\phi_1,\dots,\phi_q$.

Consider the map $f\:\Omega \to \RR^{2\cdot q}\oplus \RR^{q}=\RR^{2\cdot q,q}$
defined by
$f\:x\z\mapsto (\bm{F}_{\eps}(x),\eps\cdot\bm{\phi}(x))$.
Its induced metric tensor is $g=g+\eps\cdot h\z-\eps\cdot h$.
\qeds















\begin{thm}{Approximation theorem}\label{thm:approx}
Let $(\Omega ,g)$ be a compact Riemannian manifold.
Then there are smooth functions $\phi_1,\dots, \phi_q$ on $\Omega$ such that 
for any $\eps>0$ the metric tensor 
\[h=(d\phi_1)^2+\dots+(d\phi_q)^2\]
the following condition holds:

For any $\eps>0$, the metric tensor $g+\eps^2\cdot h$ is induced by a smooth map $\bm{F}_\eps\:\Omega \to \RR^{2\cdot q}$ to a Euclidean space.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Let $\phi_1,f_1,\dots ,\phi_q,f_q$ be the functions on $\Omega$ provided by \ref{ex:nash}.

Choose $\eps>0$. 
Consider the Nash's twist $F_i$ for each triple $(\eps,\phi_i,f_i)$.
Let
\[F_\eps=\bigoplus_i F_i,\]
that is, $F_\eps(p)=(F_1(p),\dots, F_q(p))\in \RR^2\oplus\dots\oplus\RR^2$.
Denote by $\bm{F}_\eps$ the map $\Omega \to\RR^{2\cdot q}$ with pairs of coordinate functions as in $F_1,\dots, F_q$.

By \ref{clm:twist}, the metric tensor $g+\eps^2\cdot h$ is induced by $\bm{F}_\eps$.
\qeds
