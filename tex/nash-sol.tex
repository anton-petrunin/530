\parbf{\ref{ex:n(n+1)/2}.}
Choose a point $p\in\Omega$ and a chart at $p$.

Assume that the main theorem holds for $d$.
Then components $g_{ij}=g(\partial_i,\partial_j)$ of any Riemannian metric can be written as
\[
\langle \partial_i w, \partial_j w \rangle = g_{ij},
\eqlbl{eq:<ww>=g}
\]
for some smooth map $w\:\Omega \to \RR^d$.

We have $\tbinom{n+1}{2}=n\cdot (n+1)/2$ components $g_{ij}$; each of them is a smooth function on $\Omega$.
The equation has $d$ unknowns; these are the coordinate functions of $w$.
This might convince you that $d$ should be at least $\tbinom{n+1}{2}$.
To prove this formally we will reduce the problem to an ordinary system of equtions between \emph{jet-spaces} of $w$ and $g$ at the point $p$; the jet-space is the same as space of Taylor polynomials of a given degree at $p$.

Denote by $J^m(g)$ the Taylor polynomial of $g$ at $p$ of degree $m$.
Note that this polynomial has $\tbinom{m+n}{n}$ coefficients from $n\cdot (n+1)/2$-dimensional space.
(Each coefficient is proportional to certain partial derivatives of $g$ of order at most $m$ at $p$.)
Such polinomials form an open subset in a $\tbinom{n+1}{2}\cdot\tbinom{m+n}{n}$-dimenional space.

Analogousely, let $J^m(w)$ be the Taylor polynomial of $w$ at $p$ of degree $m$.
Again, this polynomial has $\tbinom{m+n}{n}$ coefficients from $d$-dimensional space;
therefore such polinomials from a space of dimension $d\cdot\tbinom{m+n}{n}$.

The equations \ref{eq:<ww>=g} define a smooth map $J^{m+1}(w)\mapsto J^m(g)$.
In other words, any partial derivatives of $g$ of degree at most $m$ can be expressed thru partial derivatives of $w$ of degree at most $m+1$.
Since the system of equations \ref{eq:<ww>=g} have a solution for any metric $g$, there is $J^{m+1}(w)$ that maps to $J^m(g)$.
Therefore, Sard's lemma implies that
\[
d \cdot \tbinom{m+n+1}{n} \ge \tbinom{n+1}{2} \cdot  \tbinom{m+n}{n}.
\]
Since
$\tbinom{m+n+1}{n}/\tbinom{m+n}{n} \to 1$ as  $m \to \infty$, we get $d \ge \tbinom{n+1}{2}$.

\parit{Remark.} The equation \ref{eq:<ww>=g} makes perfect sense for $C^1$-smooth $w$, but in the proof we essentially used that $w$ is $C^m$-smooth for sufficiently large $m$.
Without this assumtion, the statement does not hold.

Indeed Nash--Kuiper theorem \cite{nash-1954,kuiper-1955} says that \ref{eq:<ww>=g} has $C^1$-smooth solution $w$ if $d=n+1$.
Note that this is the optimal bound as if $d=n$, then the metric $g$ is flat, which is not the case in general.

\parbf{\ref{ex:length-preserving}.} The only-if part is trivial.

Applying the length-preserving property to all arcs of curves in the coordinate directions,
show that the equality
\[g_{ii}=\langle \partial_i w, \partial_i w\rangle\]
holds almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere.

Observe that
\[2\cdot g_{ij}=g(\partial_i+\partial_j,\partial_i+\partial_j)-g(\partial_i,\partial_i)-g(\partial_j,\partial_j).\]
Using this formula with the argument above for curves in the direction $\partial_i$, $\partial_j$ and $\partial_i+\partial_j$, show that the equality
\[g_{ij}=\langle \partial_i w, \partial_j w\rangle\]
holds almost everywhere, and therefore everywhere.

\parbf{\ref{ex:torus-riemn}.}
By the definition of submanifold, we can cover $\Omega$ by a rectangular charts where $\Omega$ is written as the coordinate subspace of the first $k=\dim \Omega$ coordinates.
Note that in each chart we can present the metric on $\Omega$ as a restriction of a Riemannian metric on the whole chart.

Extend this collection of charts to an atlass of $\TT^n$ such that the images of the remaining charts do not intersect $\Omega$.
Equipp these new charts with a constant Riemannian metric.

Choose a partition of unitiy subordinate to the covering by charts and use it to mix the metrics on the charts into a metric on $\TT^n$.


\parbf{\ref{ex:short-embedding}.}
Let $\iota\:\Omega\to\RR^d$ be an embedding provided by the Whitney theorem.
Let us think that $\RR^d$ is an affine subspace $x_{d+1}=1$ in $\RR^{d+1}$.
The needed embedding can be found among maps $x\mapsto \phi\cdot\iota$, where $\phi\:\Omega\to\RR$ is a smooth positive function.

Try to write down the necessary conditions and convince yourself that there is such function $\phi$.


\parbf{\ref{ex:sum}.}
Let $\DD\subset\RR^n$ be the unit $n$-disc and $\DD^\circ$ is its interior.
Construct a smooth map $r\:\RR^n\to \SSS^n$ such that $r|_{\DD^\circ}$ is a smooth embedding
and the set $\RR^n\setminus \DD^\circ$ is mapped to the south pole.

Since $D_{\mathfrak{i}}$ is a smooth $n$-disc, there is a diffeomorphism $\iota_{\mathfrak{i}}\:\DD\to D_{\mathfrak{i}} $.
The composition $r_{\mathfrak{i}}=r\circ \iota_{\mathfrak{i}}^{-1}$ satisfies all the first two conditions.

It remains to choose a metric $h_{\mathfrak{i}}$ on $\SSS^n$ for each $\mathfrak{i}$ that meets the last condition.

Choose sufficienly small metrics $\hat h_{\mathfrak{i}}$ on $\SSS^n$ so that
\[g>\hat g=\sum_{\mathfrak{i}} r_{\mathfrak{i}}^*\hat h_{\mathfrak{i}}.\]
Let $g_1=g-\hat g$.
Choose a partition of unitiy $\phi_{\mathfrak{i}}$ that is subordant to the covering $\{D_{\mathfrak{i}}\}$;
that is, $\sum\phi_{\mathfrak{i}}=1$ and the support of $\phi_{\mathfrak{i}}$ lies in $D_{\mathfrak{i}}$ for each $\mathfrak{i}$.
Observe that $h_{\mathfrak{i}}=\hat h_{\mathfrak{i}}+\iota_{\mathfrak{i}}^*(\phi_{\mathfrak{i}}\cdot g_1)$ meets the last condition.

\parbf{\ref{ex:free-free}.}
Show and use that a smooth map $f\:\Omega\to\RR^d$ is free at $p\in\Omega$ if there is no affine subspace $A$ of dimension less than $\tfrac{n\cdot (n+3)}2$ that has \emph{the second order contact} with $f$ at $p$;
that is,
\[\rho\circ f(x)=o(|p-x|^2),\]
where $\rho(z)$ denotes the distance from $z$ to $A$.

Alternatively, express the partial derivatives in one chart thru the partial derivatives in the other chart and make a conclusion.
